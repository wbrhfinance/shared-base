Topic: The global art market, explained end to end.
Timeframe: Trailing 12 months, June 2025 to June 2026. Current state, light backward look.
Angle: Explain the whole market: segments, players, mechanics, price dynamics. Full grounding, not one thread.

Run both prompts in their own browser sessions. Paste both outputs back here for synthesis.

## PROMPT 1 — Perplexity

```
PERPLEXITY DEEP RESEARCH — Global art market
Date: 2026-06-04
Timeframe: Trailing 12 months, June 2025 to June 2026

USER ANGLE (what they want to understand):
- Explain the global art market end to end: how it is structured, who the players are, how it works.
- Map the dynamics: segments, channels, price behaviour, geographic shifts over the last 12 months.
- Surface where value moved and what the year's data actually shows.

You are a senior research analyst producing a factual sweep on the global art market.
Focus on the CURRENT STATE layer. Pair-skill ChatGPT Deep Research is
handling structural and reasoning work separately; do not duplicate
that. Your job is breadth, recency, named sources, and numbers.

Return findings under these seven headings. Use bullet points within
each. Cite every numeric or named claim with an inline source URL.

1. WHAT IT IS — A precise definition in 4 to 6 sentences. Define the
   art market scope: fine art versus decorative art and collectibles,
   primary versus secondary market, the auction / dealer / fair /
   online channels. If credible sources scope it differently, flag the
   difference.

2. CURRENT STATE — The 5 to 10 most important data points right now.
   Total market value, year-on-year change, channel split, segment
   split. Numbers with year and URL.

3. KEY PLAYERS — Top 10 named entities across the market: auction
   houses (Christie's, Sotheby's, Phillips, Bonhams, China Guardian,
   Poly), major dealers and gallery groups, fair organisers (Art Basel,
   Frieze, TEFAF), online platforms (Artsy, Artnet, LiveAuctioneers),
   data houses (Art Basel/UBS report, Artprice, ArtTactic). For each:
   one-line role, one differentiator, one URL.

4. NUMBERS AND SPECIFICATIONS — All quantitative material relevant to
   the topic for June 2025 to June 2026: total global art market value
   in USD and YoY change; aggregate auction sales (public) for the big
   houses with house-level totals where disclosed; private sales
   volumes; dealer / gallery sector value; online sales value and share
   of total; geographic shares (US, Greater China, UK, France, rest of
   EU); segment splits by medium and by price band (sub-USD 50k,
   mid-market, USD 1m-plus, USD 10m-plus); number of works sold above
   USD 10m; ultra-contemporary segment value; art fair count, attendance
   and reported sales; art-secured lending volumes. Give ranges where
   the market varies. Year and source for every figure.

5. RECENT DEVELOPMENTS (last 90 days) — Material news: marquee auction
   results, house restructurings or leadership changes, fair launches or
   cancellations, platform moves, regulatory or tax changes affecting
   the trade. Date + one-line + URL.

6. NARRATIVE — How is the art market being discussed in credible press,
   trade publications, and the major market reports right now? Soft,
   recovering, bifurcating high-end versus mid-market, generational
   handover, geographic rebalancing? 3 to 5 sentences with
   representative source URLs.

7. OPEN QUESTIONS — Three to five questions where the public record
   is thin, contradictory, or absent (e.g. true size of private sales,
   real online share, Chinese market opacity). These will be passed to
   the reasoning engine.

SOURCE STANDARDS
- Tier 1: primary documents (auction house financial disclosures and
  results, the Art Basel and UBS Global Art Market Report, TEFAF Art
  Market Report, regulatory filings, official fair data), peer-reviewed,
  top trade press.
- Tier 2: established analyst and data houses (Artprice, ArtTactic,
  Artnet Intelligence, Deloitte Art & Finance), tier-1 financial press.
- Tier 3: gallery blogs, art-market Substacks, forums — useful for
  recency but tag as such.
- Aim for 15+ distinct sources. No single source anchors more than
  three claims.
- Where two reputable sources disagree, show both with the
  disagreement explicit.

OUTPUT RULES
- Distinguish: (a) confirmed fact, (b) widely-cited consensus, (c)
  emerging signal. Tag each material claim.
- No essay prose. Headings, bullets, citations.
- If a figure is not available, write "UNKNOWN — public record thin"
  rather than inventing or estimating.
- Plain English. No em dashes.

Save as: Perplexity_art_market.txt
```

## PROMPT 2 — ChatGPT Deep Research

```
Conduct deep research on the global art market and produce a structured
explainer as of 2026-06-04. Horizon: trailing 12 months, June 2025 to
June 2026, with a near-term forward look over the next 12 months.

USER ANGLE (what they want to understand):
- Explain the global art market end to end: how it is structured, who
  the players are, how it works.
- Map the dynamics: segments, channels, price behaviour, geographic
  shifts over the last 12 months.
- Surface where value moved and what the year's data actually shows.

Pair-skill Perplexity is handling current-state, named-source, numeric
breadth separately. Your job is structure, reasoning, mechanism, and
forward view. Do not duplicate breadth work; go deeper on the layers
below.

Produce these eight sections in order. Every figure carries a year and
a source URL.

1. BOTTOM LINE (5 sentences). What the art market is in plain English,
   why it matters, and the single most important thing someone learning
   it should understand about how it works.

2. HOW IT WORKS — Mechanism and structure. The chain from artist to
   collector: primary market (galleries, dealers) and secondary market
   (auctions, private sales, resale). How price is set and discovered,
   the role of provenance, the function of fairs, the information
   asymmetry between buyer and seller. One short paragraph per node.

3. WHY IT IS SHAPED THE WAY IT IS — The underlying drivers. Why the
   market is opaque, why supply is fixed and demand status-driven, the
   role of scarcity and authentication, the tax and freeport regime, the
   concentration of value at the top. The thing that, if it changed,
   would change everything else.

4. APPROACHES AND MODELS — The distinct channels and ownership models
   in the market. Seed set: public auction, private treaty sales,
   primary gallery / dealer, art fairs, online-only platforms, and
   fractional / tokenised ownership. For each: who uses it, what
   trade-offs it makes (price discovery versus discretion, reach versus
   cost, liquidity versus control), when it wins, when it loses.

5. TRADE-OFFS AND LEVERS — The decisions participants make: buy at
   auction versus privately, sell now versus hold, primary versus
   secondary, the cost of guarantees and irrevocable bids, buyer's and
   seller's premiums, art as an asset versus art as consumption. What is
   gained and lost on each.

6. WHAT COULD CHANGE — Near-term forward look over the next 12 months.
   The 3 to 5 developments most likely to shift the market materially.
   Seed candidates: interest-rate path and its effect on discretionary
   spend, the generational handover of collections and estate supply,
   the high-end versus mid-market split widening, online and platform
   consolidation, Chinese market trajectory, AI-generated work and
   authentication technology. Be concrete: events, named actors,
   observable signals.

7. COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS — Three to five things people get wrong about
   the art market (e.g. that headline auction records reflect the whole
   market, that art is a reliable inflation hedge, that the market is
   liquid), with the correction and why the misconception persists.

8. WHAT I DON'T KNOW — Three to five honest gaps where the public
   record is thin or contradictory (private sales true size, Chinese
   market reporting, real online share, dealer revenues). Pair-skill
   Perplexity is asked the same question; the union sets the next
   research agenda.

OUTPUT RULES
- Distinguish: (a) confirmed fact, (b) widely-cited consensus, (c)
  emerging signal. Tag each material claim.
- Minimum 20 high-quality sources with URLs.
- Where sources conflict, surface the conflict and which view you
  lean toward, with reasoning.
- Plain prose. Short paragraphs. No bullet padding. No em dashes.
- Do not produce a methodology essay, a "how to research this topic"
  document, a workflow proposal, or a list of clarifying questions.
  Execute the research above and produce the eight-section
  deliverable.

Save as: ChatGPT_art_market.txt or .docx
```
